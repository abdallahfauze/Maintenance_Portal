# 7. Partnership Framework — Working With Contractor Partners

Your entire supply side depends on getting this right. This document covers how to find, vet, sign, onboard, pay, and manage contractor partners, plus what belongs in the actual partnership agreement (**the agreement itself must be drafted/reviewed by a licensed Saudi lawyer** — this document tells you what it needs to cover, not the enforceable legal language).

## 7.1 What kind of partner you're looking for

Target **small-to-mid-size maintenance/trade companies that already have technicians stationed in your target cities**, not individual freelancers. Why:

- They already hold (or can readily hold) the correct municipal/Balady and, where relevant, Civil Defense and SCA licensing — this is their legal existence, not something we grant them.
- They already carry (or can be required to obtain) liability insurance.
- They have existing technician management, payroll, and supervision — you are not building an HR function for gig workers.
- A company is easier to hold accountable (via contract, deposit/holdback, and reputational consequences) than an individual.

**Look for**: companies already doing electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or general maintenance work for villas/compounds/facility management contracts in your target cities — they have exactly the footprint you need, and the app gives them a new demand channel without them needing to build one themselves.

## 7.2 The recruitment funnel

1. **Sourcing** — direct outreach (this is largely relationship-driven in KSA; industry associations, municipal contractor directories, referrals from one good partner to another, and even competitor-app technician sightings — "who does this van belong to?" — are all legitimate sourcing channels).
2. **Initial screening call/meeting** — trade focus, city coverage, number of technicians, years operating, willingness to the commission model.
3. **Document verification** (do this before signing anything):
   - Commercial Registration (verify live status via the **Wathq** business-verification platform)
   - Municipal/Balady trade license matching the claimed trade
   - Civil Defense approval, where applicable to their premises/activity
   - Proof of liability insurance (or a commitment to obtain it as a condition of onboarding)
   - GOSI registration (confirms they're actually running payroll for their technicians, not using undocumented labor)
4. **Sample work/reference check** — ask for 2-3 recent customer references, or if feasible, have a team member book and observe a real job before signing.
5. **Contract signing** (see §7.3).
6. **Onboarding & training** — how the app/dispatch system works, your pricing bands, your SLA expectations, your dispute process.
7. **Probation period** — first 30-60 days and first ~10-20 jobs on a shorter review cycle before they're treated as a fully trusted, high-volume partner.

## 7.3 What the partnership agreement needs to cover (brief for your lawyer)

| Section | What it should establish |
|---|---|
| **Parties & term** | Who's who, contract duration, renewal, and termination conditions (including a clear, fast "for cause" termination for safety violations or repeated poor performance) |
| **Scope of work** | Which trades/services the contractor is approved to accept jobs for through the platform |
| **Commission & payment terms** | The commission rate (§6.5 of the [Financial Plan](06-Financial-Plan.md)), payment schedule (e.g., weekly payout), and what happens on customer refunds/disputes |
| **Performance standards / SLA** | Response time commitments, completion time, no-show policy — cross-reference [SLA Templates](08-SLA-Templates.md) |
| **Licensing & insurance warranties** | Contractor warrants it holds and will maintain all required licenses and insurance, and must notify us immediately if any lapse |
| **Non-circumvention clause** | A clause discouraging the contractor from taking a platform-sourced customer off-platform to avoid commission (understand this is difficult to enforce in practice — see [Risk Analysis](05-Risk-Analysis.md) §5.1 — but it sets a clear contractual expectation and is standard in every marketplace agreement) |
| **Quality & conduct standards** | Technician conduct at customer homes (ID badge worn, no smoking inside, respectful of customer property/family — important trust factors), safety-standard compliance |
| **Deposit/holdback mechanism** | A modest, refundable security holdback (e.g., withheld from early payouts) that can be drawn on for verified customer damage claims — protects your guarantee/refund policy without you carrying 100% of that risk yourself |
| **Data protection** | Contractor's obligations regarding customer personal data (address, access details) they receive through the platform, consistent with our PDPL obligations |
| **Liability & indemnity** | Who is responsible for what in case of property damage, injury, or a botched job — this needs real legal drafting, not a template |
| **Exclusivity (or lack of it)** | Almost certainly **non-exclusive** at launch (contractors keep their existing business alongside us) — exclusivity is a Phase 2+ negotiation once you're providing them significant volume |
| **Dispute resolution** | Governing law (Saudi law), and the process/venue for resolving disagreements |

## 7.4 Ongoing partner management

- **Score every partner** on: on-time rate, completion rate, average customer rating, dispute rate, and cancellation rate. Review monthly.
- **Tiering**: consider a simple Bronze/Silver/Gold tier tied to performance — better-performing partners get priority job routing and, eventually, a small commission discount. This creates a genuine incentive loop beyond the contract's stick-based clauses.
- **A real three-strikes policy**: document specific trigger events (safety violation, no-show without notice, verified customer complaint of theft/damage, license lapse) and enforce it consistently — the app's entire value proposition collapses if bad actors are tolerated for revenue reasons.
- **Regular check-ins**: don't just manage partners through the app dashboard — a human relationship (calls, periodic in-person visits) keeps you informed of problems before they show up as bad reviews.

## 7.5 Coverage-planning target for Phase 1 launch

| City | Trades | Minimum partner companies per trade (redundancy target) |
|---|---|---|
| Jeddah (launch city) | Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC, Civil finishes | 4-5 each |
| Riyadh (Phase 1, month 7+) | Same | 4-5 each |
| Dammam (Phase 1, month 10+) | Same | 3-4 each (smaller market) |

Redundancy matters: if you only have one electrical partner in a city and they have a bad week, every electrical customer in that city has a bad experience. Never launch a trade in a city with only a single partner.

## 7.6 What this plan can and can't do here

This framework tells you exactly what to look for, verify, and negotiate. It **cannot** draft the enforceable contract language, verify a specific company's license status for you (you must check Wathq/Balady yourself or through your lawyer), or negotiate on your behalf. Bring this document to a Saudi corporate/commercial lawyer as the brief for drafting your actual Partnership Agreement template.
