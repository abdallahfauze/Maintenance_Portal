# 6. Financial Plan

**Important framing before the numbers**: every figure below is a **planning-grade estimate**, built from comparable-business logic and current KSA market data (cited where used) — not a quote from any vendor. Treat this as the *structure* of your budget (what categories exist, how they relate to each other, what a realistic ratio between them looks like), and replace each line with a real quote as you get one. All figures in SAR unless noted (1 USD ≈ 3.75 SAR).

## 6.1 The two-stage funding approach (recommended)

Rather than raising and spending one large lump sum, we recommend funding this in two stages — this is the single biggest way to reduce your financial risk (see [Risk Analysis](05-Risk-Analysis.md) §5.4):

| Stage | Purpose | Capital needed | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 0 — Lean Validation** | Prove real customer and contractor demand before big spend, using a simple landing page + WhatsApp Business booking + a 2-3 person manual dispatch team, in Jeddah only | **SAR 80,000 – 120,000** | 2–3 months |
| **Stage 1 — Full 3-City Buildout** | Full app (customer + contractor + admin), proper team, launch across Jeddah → Riyadh → Dammam, plus enough operating runway to reach break-even | **SAR 900,000 – 1,300,000** (one-time build ≈ 550,000–650,000 + operating runway to break-even ≈ 350,000–650,000) | Months 3–18 |

**Only commit to Stage 1 if Stage 0 shows real signal** (a landing page converting visitors to bookings, contractors willing to sign on, jobs actually getting completed at acceptable quality). This is the difference between spending SAR 100,000 to learn something versus spending SAR 1,000,000+ to learn the same thing the hard way.

## 6.5 Pricing structure — how money actually flows

### Revenue streams and suggested starting rates

| Stream | Suggested starting rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Commission on completed job | **18%** of job value (adjustable 15-25% by job size — lower % on large jobs to stay competitive, since your value-add per riyal is smaller on a SAR 2,000 renovation than a SAR 150 socket fix) | Validate with real contractor conversations during onboarding — no competitor publishes their rate, so treat this as a starting hypothesis, not a fixed law |
| Customer booking/dispatch fee | **SAR 20** flat per booking | Discourages no-shows; waived or reduced for subscription members |
| "Portal Care" maintenance subscription | **SAR 249/year** (AC-only plan: 2 services/year) or **SAR 599/year** (Complete Home: AC + annual electrical/plumbing safety check + priority booking + 10% parts discount) | Highest-margin line once trust is established — push actively after every completed job |
| Contractor featured placement (Phase 2+) | Optional, once demand > supply in a city | Do not introduce until contractors are already getting steady volume — otherwise it just feels like an extra tax |

### A worked example

A SAR 350 socket-and-breaker job: customer pays **SAR 370** (350 + SAR 20 booking fee). We keep **SAR 63** commission (18% of 350) + the SAR 20 fee = **SAR 83** to us; the contractor receives **SAR 287**.

## 6.6 Illustrative path to break-even (Year 1)

This is a simplified model to show the *shape* of the ramp, not a precise forecast — actual numbers depend heavily on real CAC and contractor density achieved.

| Month | City coverage | Est. jobs/month | Est. net revenue (commission + fees) | Running cost | Monthly gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Jeddah soft launch | 50 | ~4,150 | 72,000 | -67,850 |
| 6 | Jeddah ramping | 500 | ~41,500 | 98,000 | -56,500 |
| 9 | + Riyadh | 1,400 | ~116,200 | 137,000 | -20,800 |
| 12 | + Dammam | 2,800 | ~232,400 | 171,000 | **+61,400** |

On this illustrative model, **monthly operating break-even lands around month 10-12**, with the subscription revenue stream (not modeled in the table above, as it starts small) providing further upside beyond this. **Full payback of the initial capital invested** (recovering the ~SAR 550,000-650,000 one-time build cost plus the operating losses incurred while ramping) realistically lands around **month 18-24** — this is why the Stage 0/Stage 1 phased funding approach in §6.1 matters so much: you want to know the ramp is real before you're carrying that much cumulative risk.

## 6.7 Financing options

| Option | What it is | Fit for this business |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-funded / founder capital** | You fund Stage 0 yourself | Best for Stage 0 specifically — keeps early equity clean and proves the model before external money is needed |
| **Angel/seed investment** | External investors take equity | Natural fit for Stage 1, once Stage 0 has real validation data to show |
| **Kafalah SME Loan Guarantee Program** | A Saudi government program (via Monsha'at) that guarantees a portion of an SME loan to a participating bank, making banks more willing to lend to a new company without a long track record | Worth exploring for Stage 1 debt financing — reduces reliance on giving up equity; requires working with a bank that participates in the Kafalah program |
| **Monsha'at broader SME support** | Non-financial support: training, mentorship, market-access programs | Low-cost, high-value — engage with Monsha'at early regardless of your financing choice |

## 6.8 What this plan can and can't do on financials

This plan gives you a **realistic, internally consistent budgeting framework** you can hand to a bank, investor, or accountant as a starting structure. It **cannot** replace a licensed accountant's statutory financial statements, a real vendor quote, or a bank's own credit assessment. Before you raise money on these numbers, get: (1) at least 3 real app-development quotes, (2) a licensed accountant to build this into a proper 3-statement model (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow) with your actual chosen legal structure and tax treatment, and (3) real contractor conversations on commission tolerance.
