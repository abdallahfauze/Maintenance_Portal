# 8. SLA Templates — Service Level Agreements

An SLA (Service Level Agreement) is simply a written promise: "here's exactly what you can expect, and here's what happens if we don't deliver it." You need SLAs in **two directions**: what you promise the customer, and what you require from the contractor to be able to keep that promise. Use these as a drafting brief — final wording should be reviewed by your lawyer before publishing.

## 8.1 Customer-facing SLA (what we promise the customer)

| Commitment | Standard tier | Priority tier (subscription members) |
|---|---|---|
| Booking confirmation | Within 15 minutes of request | Within 5 minutes |
| Technician dispatch window (non-emergency) | Same day or next day, scheduled slot | Same day, priority slot |
| Emergency response (burst pipe, total power loss, gas smell — safety-relevant) | Within 2-4 hours, where a partner is available | Within 1-2 hours |
| Technician arrival window accuracy | Within a 2-hour window communicated in advance | Within a 1-hour window |
| Upfront price estimate | Provided before technician dispatch for standard job types | Same, plus locked-in pricing for subscription-covered services |
| Post-job guarantee/warranty | 30 days on workmanship for the same issue, at no extra charge | 60 days |
| Cancellation notice | Customer may cancel free of charge up to 1 hour before scheduled arrival | Up to 30 minutes |
| Response to a complaint | Acknowledged within 4 business hours, resolved or escalated within 48 hours | Within 2 business hours / 24 hours |
| Refund on verified no-show or unresolved issue | Full refund of booking fee + job cost if unresolved after warranty visit | Same, expedited |

**Escalation ladder for customers**: in-app chat/rating → phone support → formal complaint with a case number and resolution deadline → refund/compensation per policy. Publish this ladder in the app so customers know what to expect — ambiguity is what turns a single bad job into a public one-star review.

## 8.2 Contractor-facing SLA (what we require from partners)

| Requirement | Standard | Consequence of breach |
|---|---|---|
| Job acceptance/decline response time | Within 10 minutes of dispatch | Job auto-routed to next available partner; repeated slow response affects tier ranking |
| Arrival within the promised window | Required, with proactive in-app notice if delayed | Late-arrival penalty (commission discount on that job) after a grace period; repeated lateness triggers review |
| No-show | Not permitted without ≥2 hours' notice | Immediate review; second occurrence in a rolling 90 days = probation; third = termination |
| Technician identification | Must wear/display platform ID badge and carry company ID | Job can be refused by customer; contractor is responsible for retraining |
| Upfront pricing adherence | Must quote within the platform's published price band for standard job types, or clearly justify and get customer approval for any deviation before starting work | Price disputes reviewed case-by-case; repeated overcharging = removal |
| Workmanship warranty compliance | Must return to fix the same issue free of charge within the warranty period if it recurs | Cost of the warranty visit is not billable to us or the customer; contractor absorbs it as their standard trade practice |
| Safety & licensing | Must maintain valid municipal/Civil Defense/SCA licensing and required insurance at all times, and self-report any lapse | Immediate suspension pending resolution |
| Property care | No property damage beyond the job scope; any damage must be reported immediately, not discovered by the customer later | Damage claims draw first from the contractor's security holdback (see [Partnership Framework](07-Partnership-Framework.md) §7.3); repeated incidents = termination |
| Conduct | Professional, respectful conduct in customer homes; no smoking indoors; respect for privacy | Any verified harassment/theft complaint = immediate suspension pending investigation, likely termination |

## 8.3 Trade-specific SLA notes

| Trade | Special considerations |
|---|---|
| **Electrical** | Safety-critical — any job touching a main panel/breaker box should require the technician to test and confirm power is properly isolated before working; a documented safety checklist per job is worth requiring |
| **Plumbing** | Water-damage risk is the main customer fear — require contractors to confirm the water supply is properly shut off/isolated before starting, and to leave the work area dry and cleaned |
| **HVAC** | Seasonal surge (see [Risk Analysis](05-Risk-Analysis.md) §5.6) means summer SLA windows may need to be explicitly wider ("same-day" becomes "within 24-48 hours during peak summer") — communicate this transparently rather than over-promising and failing |
| **Civil finishes (paint/tiling)** | Longer jobs (multi-day) need a different SLA shape — milestone-based check-ins rather than a single arrival-window promise; set clear expectations on furniture-covering/dust protection |

## 8.4 Why this two-sided structure matters

The customer-facing SLA is your **marketing promise** — it's what makes people trust the app enough to book a stranger into their home. The contractor-facing SLA is the **operational mechanism that makes that promise deliverable**. If you publish generous customer promises without contractor-side enforcement to back them, you will consistently break your own promises and destroy trust faster than a competitor with no promises at all. Build and enforce the contractor SLA *before* marketing the customer SLA aggressively.

## 8.5 What this plan can and can't do here

These tables are a complete, ready-to-use **content and structure brief**. They are not yet a signed legal document — hand this to your lawyer to convert into the binding Terms of Service (customer-facing) and Partnership Agreement schedule (contractor-facing), and to your insurance broker to confirm what's actually coverable (particularly the warranty and property-damage provisions).
