# 3. Market Research

## 3.1 The market size, in plain numbers

| City | Metro-area population (~2025) | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Riyadh | ~7.95 million | Phase 1 |
| Jeddah | ~4.7 million | Phase 1 |
| Dammam–Khobar–Dhahran conurbation | ~1.38 million | Phase 1 |
| Madinah, Taif, Jubail | Mid-size cities, each in the several-hundred-thousand to ~1.5M range | Phase 2 |

*Sources: World Population Review, Macrotrends city population series, GlobalMediaInsight Saudi population statistics 2026 (see citations at the bottom of this document). Household-count data (a more precise proxy for "number of homes needing maintenance") was not available at city level in open sources during this research — GASTAT (the General Authority for Statistics) publishes household survey data periodically; **getting the latest GASTAT household count per city is a cheap, high-value next step** before you finalize sizing for a bank or investor deck.*

**Rule-of-thumb sizing**: Saudi household size averages roughly 5–6 people nationally (higher than most Western markets), so Phase 1's ~14 million combined metro population implies **roughly 2.3–2.8 million households** across Jeddah, Riyadh, and Dammam. Even a conservative assumption that 1% of households book one paid maintenance job per month through the app represents 23,000–28,000 jobs/month at scale — useful as a "sky's the limit" ceiling, not a Year-1 forecast (see [Financial Plan](06-Financial-Plan.md) for realistic Year-1 volume assumptions).

## 3.2 Why demand exists right now

- **Vision 2030 housing push**: home-ownership targets and new residential development have significantly grown the stock of owner-occupied villas and apartments, which age and need maintenance on a predictable cycle (AC units, water heaters, plumbing fixtures all have known service lifespans).
- **Climate reality**: HVAC is not optional in Saudi Arabia — air conditioning maintenance is one of the highest-frequency, highest-willingness-to-pay categories, especially before and during summer peak (a strong seasonal marketing hook).
- **Urban lifestyle shift**: dual-income households and a younger, more app-native population increasingly prefer booking a vetted technician on a phone over calling around for word-of-mouth referrals or flagging down a random "sabbak" (handyman) truck.
- **Labor market structure**: most residential trade labor in KSA is performed by expatriate technicians employed by small-to-mid maintenance companies, not by individual freelance citizens (freelance licensing — the "Maroof"/Freelance Work Document route — is restricted to Saudi nationals and covers ~120 approved professions; it is **not** the mechanism most electrical/plumbing/HVAC technicians would use). This is exactly why the "partner with existing companies" model fits the actual labor market structure, rather than fighting it.

## 3.3 Who's already in this market (competitive landscape)

This is a real, active market — not a blank slate. Current or recent players identified in this research include:

| Player | What they appear to do | What we can learn / where the gap is |
|---|---|---|
| **MAHA** | On-demand home maintenance & property services app; electrical, plumbing, AC, painting, carpentry | Multi-trade like our plan — a direct comparable to study closely (download the app, book a test job if possible, read App Store/Google Play reviews for complaint patterns) |
| **HomeXA** | Professional home & facility maintenance booking platform | Also multi-trade; appears to target both homes and buildings/facilities — worth benchmarking their B2B angle for our Phase 2 corporate expansion |
| **Fix Riyadh Now** | Riyadh-specific 24/7 electrical & plumbing for residential buildings | Single-city, single-trade-pair focus — suggests city-specific players exist and a multi-city, multi-trade platform is a genuine differentiator |
| **Ajeer** | AC, plumbing, electrical maintenance "plans" nationwide | Direct comparable; their "plans" language supports our subscription revenue stream idea |
| **Noon Home Services** | Home services vertical from Noon (major KSA e-commerce platform) | A well-capitalized, trusted consumer brand entering the space is a signal the category is validated — but also a serious long-term competitive threat given Noon's brand recognition and logistics muscle |
| **Albaap** | Home services app | Another established competitor to review |
| **Muheel (Abdul Latif Jameel)** | App-based maintenance service launched by a major, long-established Saudi conglomerate | Signals institutional/corporate confidence in this category; also a well-funded competitor to watch |
| **"Plumber & Electrician" (regional apps, e.g. Al-Kharj)** | Smaller, hyper-local single-city apps | Shows the market is fragmented enough for small local players to exist — reinforces that no one has "won" multi-city trust yet |

**What this tells us**: the category is validated (multiple funded players, including one backed by a major conglomerate), but it is fragmented across single-city or single-trade players, with even the multi-trade national players not obviously dominant or trusted as a household name yet. **The opening is not "invent a new category" — it's "execute better than a fragmented field on trust, SLA reliability, and multi-city + multi-trade convenience."** Before finalizing your go-to-market plan, we strongly recommend a hands-on competitive audit: download MAHA, HomeXA, Ajeer, and Noon Home Services, book at least one real test job each if feasible, and log price, response time, and technician experience — this is qualitative work no desk research can substitute for.

## 3.4 Customer segments and what each one wants

| Segment | What they care about most | Implication for us |
|---|---|---|
| Villa owners (often families, higher income) | Reliability, trust, a known technician for recurring jobs, avoiding damage/theft risk from letting a stranger in | Emphasize vetting, ID verification, ratings, and a subscription/recurring relationship |
| Apartment renters/tenants | Speed, price transparency, minimal hassle (may not want to negotiate with landlord over cost) | Emphasize instant upfront pricing and fast response time |
| Expatriate households | English-language support, digital payment convenience, unfamiliarity with local informal-referral networks (so more reliant on an app) | Strong bilingual UX; a key early-adopter segment, since they're less likely to have an existing trusted "guy" |
| Landlords managing multiple units | Volume, consolidated invoicing, consistent quality across scattered properties | A natural bridge segment toward the eventual corporate/facility offering |

## 3.5 Seasonality and demand patterns to plan around

- **HVAC** demand spikes hard from ~March through September (AC servicing, breakdowns during peak heat) — this is your single best marketing and cash-flow-planning driver.
- **Electrical and plumbing** are more evenly distributed year-round but see a bump around Ramadan/Eid and school-year-start as households prepare their homes for guests/family visits.
- **Civil finishes (paint/tiling)** tend to cluster around move-in/move-out cycles and just before major holidays.

Plan Phase 1 launch timing to land **before the summer AC season** if at all possible — being the app people already trust when the first heatwave hits is a major acquisition advantage.

## 3.6 Where this research is strong vs. where you should spend more before finalizing investor materials

**Strong / usable as-is**: regulatory landscape, competitor identification, city-level population sizing, revenue-model precedent, seasonality.

**Needs primary research before a bank/investor pitch**:
- Exact household counts per city (GASTAT direct request or purchase of a market report)
- Mystery-shopping actual competitor pricing and response times
- Direct conversations with 10–15 candidate contractor companies on what commission % and payment terms they'd actually accept (desk research could not find published commission rates for any competitor)
- A simple customer survey/landing-page smoke test (a "coming soon, join the waitlist" page with paid social ads is a cheap way to measure real demand before writing a large check for app development — this is expanded on in [Recommendations & Roadmap](09-Roadmap-and-Recommendations.md))
